Every rep in my class has 2 halves. The lift. And the let-go.

I time the let-go at **twice** the lift.

Here is why.

## A muscle that stays tight gets tired

Make a fist and hold it all day. By evening your hand aches, and your grip is weak.

Your pelvic floor works the same way. The International Continence Society lists relaxation as part of normal pelvic floor function, and rates it as full, partial or delayed.<Ref n={1} />

Some pelvic problems come from too much tension. A systematic review found signs of raised tone or overactive pelvic floor muscles across several pelvic health conditions.<Ref n={2} />

For those women, more squeezing adds to the problem. A better let-go is the fix.

## Signs your pelvic floor may hold too much tension

- Pain with sex, or pain when you insert a tampon.
- Trouble starting to pee, or a slow stream.
- A bladder that still feels full after you go.
- Constipation, with straining.
- Pelvic, low back or hip pain that gets worse after Kegels.

Any of these? See a doctor or a pelvic floor physical therapist **before** you add more squeezes.

## Quality beats volume

"100 Kegels a day" is a myth. Guidelines set a floor of 8 contractions, 3 times a day.<Ref n={3} /> Each one with a full let-go.

24 good reps beat 100 rushed ones.

<Try title="the 2-minute let-go drill" cat="sleep">

1. Lie on your back with a pillow under your knees.
2. Breathe in through your nose for 4 seconds. Let your belly rise. Picture your pelvic floor softening, like a hammock taking weight.
3. Breathe out for 6 seconds. Do nothing else.
4. Scan from the top: jaw soft, shoulders heavy, belly soft, pelvic floor soft.

Repeat for 8 breaths.

</Try>

## How to build it into your Kegels

- **Weeks 1 and 2:** lift for 2 seconds, let go for 4.
- **As holds grow to 5 to 10 seconds:** let go for at least as long as you held.
- **After each set:** 1 extra breath, only for letting go.

The app's player works this way. A breathing ring grows on the in-breath, while you let go, and shrinks on the out-breath, while you lift.

<Remember>

1. Relaxing is half of the training.
2. Let go for at least as long as you lift, and twice as long while you learn.
3. Pain, a slow stream or pain with sex: see a doctor or pelvic floor physical therapist before you add more squeezes.

</Remember>
