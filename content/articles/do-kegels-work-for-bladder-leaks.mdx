A Cochrane review pools the best trials on one question. This number comes from 4 trials and 165 women, after 3 to 6 months of training.<Ref n={1} />

## Cure is the high bar. Improvement is wider.

Count the women who were cured **or** improved, and it was 74% with training against 11% without (3 trials, 242 women).<Ref n={1} />

Women who trained also had about 1 fewer leak every 24 hours (7 trials, 432 women).<Ref n={1} />

## Why every guideline puts it first

- **NICE, UK:** at least 3 months of supervised pelvic floor training, first, for stress or mixed leaks.<Ref n={2} />
- **ACOG, US:** pelvic floor training is effective first-line care for stress, urgency and mixed leaks.<Ref n={3} />
- **AUA, US:** the American Urological Association lists pelvic floor exercises as a non-surgical option.<Ref n={4} />

First-line means: start here, before pills or procedures.

## Does an app work?

A Swedish trial gave 123 women with stress leaks either a training app or nothing for 3 months.<Ref n={5} /> 55.7% of app users rated themselves much improved. In the other group, 5% did.

Here is the catch. At 3 months, 41% of app users still trained every day.<Ref n={5} />

**The method works. The habit is the hard part.**
