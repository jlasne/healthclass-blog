Pregnancy puts 9 months of growing load on your pelvic floor. Then birth stretches it.

You can train for that, the way you would train for a long hike.

## What the evidence shows

A Cochrane review pooled 11 trials and 3,307 pregnant women.<Ref n={1} />

Women who did pelvic floor training during pregnancy were **22% less likely** to report leaks in late pregnancy. The authors rate the evidence as moderate quality.

## Timing matters

The same review found weaker results for blanket training programs offered to every woman after birth.<Ref n={1} />

So my advice: start during pregnancy, while your pelvic floor is still building toward the big day.

<Doctor title="Check with your doctor first if">

- You have bleeding, placenta problems or a risk of preterm labor.
- You had pelvic, belly or vaginal surgery in the last 3 months.
- You have pelvic pain, or pain with sex.
- You see or feel a bulge at the vaginal opening.

</Doctor>

## How to train during pregnancy

The basics match any Kegel:

1. Lift on the out-breath, as if holding in gas.
2. Let go fully on the in-breath.
3. 8 lifts, 3 times a day.<Ref n={2} />

What changes with the bump:

- **Positions.** As your belly grows, train sitting, lying on your side, or on hands and knees.
- **Effort.** Gentle to firm. Breathe through every rep.
- **The let-go.** It matters even more now. For a vaginal birth, your pelvic floor needs to soften and stretch. Practice the let-go as carefully as the lift.

<Try title="side-lying lifts" cat="sleep">

Lie on your left side, a pillow between your knees. Breathe out and lift for 3 seconds. Breathe in and let go for 6 seconds.

8 reps, twice a day.

</Try>

## After birth

If you gave birth in the last 6 weeks, or had a cesarean, check with your doctor before you train. Then start gently, with the let-go first, and rebuild the lift over the following weeks.
