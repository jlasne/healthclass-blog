Picture a hammock.

It stretches from your pubic bone in front to your tailbone in back. Your bladder, your uterus and your bowel rest on it.

That hammock is your pelvic floor: a group of muscles at the base of your pelvis, the bowl of bones between your hips.

It has 3 jobs.

## Job 1: hold everything up

When you stand, cough or lift a toddler, the pressure in your belly rises. The hammock takes that load.

A firm, springy hammock keeps your organs where they belong.

## Job 2: keep the exits closed, then open them

3 openings pass through the hammock: the urethra (the short tube that carries urine out), the vagina and the anus.

The muscles wrap these exits. They squeeze to keep things in. They relax to let things out.

**Both halves matter.** The International Continence Society counts relaxing as part of normal pelvic floor function, right next to squeezing.<Ref n={2} />

<Letter />

## Job 3: sex

The same muscles play a part in sensation and orgasm. They also need to relax for comfortable sex.

Pain during sex is common. It is still worth a visit to your doctor.

## It moves with your breath

On an in-breath, your diaphragm and your pelvic floor both move down. On an out-breath, both move up.<Ref n={1} />

That finding comes from MRI scans of 8 healthy women. So treat it as a useful picture from a small study.

It is also why I teach the lift on the out-breath.

<Try title="find your hammock in 60 seconds">

1. Lie on your back, knees bent, feet flat.
2. Breathe in for 4 seconds. Let your belly and your pelvic floor soften.
3. Breathe out for 6 seconds. As you do, gently lift, as if you were holding in gas.
4. Let it all go on the next in-breath.

Do this 5 times at half effort. Still unsure? Try lying on your side. Feeling it is a skill, and skills grow with practice.

</Try>

## Why this matters for leaks

A leak happens when the pressure on your bladder beats the hammock's hold. You have 2 ways to win:

1. A stronger, faster hammock. That is training.
2. A hammock that lifts **before** the pressure hits. That is [the Knack](/letters/the-knack-squeeze-before-you-sneeze).

Pelvic floor muscle training is the first treatment guidelines recommend for stress leaks.<Ref n={3} /> It starts with knowing where the muscle is. Now you do.
