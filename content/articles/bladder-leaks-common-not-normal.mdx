6 in 10 American women leaked urine in the past year.<Ref n={1} />

That number comes from a national survey of 5,006 women. It makes leaks one of the most common health issues women have.

Here is the part I want you to keep: common and normal are two different words. Gray hair is normal. A leak when you laugh is a signal. A muscle is asking for training.

**And muscles answer to training.**

**Stress leaks** happen when pressure lands on your bladder: a laugh, a sneeze, a jump, a heavy bag.

**Urgency leaks** come with a sudden "I have to go now" feeling, often on the way to the bathroom.

**Mixed leaks** are both. Add mixed to stress, and 68.8% of women who leak have some stress leaks.

Your type decides your first step. So find it first.

## Leaks rise with age. So does the value of starting now.

In another national survey, 9.7% of women aged 20 to 39 had a pelvic floor disorder. At 80 and older, the number was 49.7%.<Ref n={2} />

Age adds load to the muscles. Training adds strength back. The best year to start is the year you notice it.

## The first step I recommend

Pelvic floor muscle training. Every major guideline puts it first for stress and mixed leaks: NICE in the UK, and ACOG in the US.<Ref n={3} /><Ref n={4} />

It has the numbers behind it. In a Cochrane review, 56% of women with stress leaks who trained reported cure. Without training, 6% did.<Ref n={5} />

It costs nothing. It takes 5 minutes a day. You can do it on your couch.

<Try title="the 1-week leak log">

For 7 days, write each leak in 2 words: what you were doing, and when. "Laughing, 3 pm." "Rushing to the bathroom, 7 am."

After a week, your type is clear. Your doctor gets a head start too.

</Try>

- You see blood in your urine, feel burning when you pee, or have a fever.
- You see or feel a bulge at the vaginal opening.
- You struggle to start peeing, or your bladder still feels full after you go.
- Your leaks started after surgery, radiation or a new medicine.
