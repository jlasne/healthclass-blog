## Why 3 months?

The trials that show pelvic floor training works ran 3 to 6 months.<Ref n={1} /> So NICE asks for at least 3 months of training as the first treatment for stress or mixed leaks. For prolapse, it asks for at least 16 weeks.<Ref n={2} />

My programs run 6 guided weeks. Week 6 hands you a routine to carry to week 12 and past it.

## The daily dose

- **At least 8 contractions, 3 times a day.** That is the NICE floor.<Ref n={2} />
- **About 24 a day, for 6 weeks or more.** In a meta-analysis, benefits stopped growing past that dose.<Ref n={3} />
- **More training days per week may help.** The evidence here is low certainty.<Ref n={4} />

## What to expect, stage by stage

- **Weeks 1 and 2, find it.** The lift becomes clear. Holds of 2 to 3 seconds.
- **Weeks 3 and 4, hold it.** 5-second holds. Add [the Knack](/letters/the-knack-squeeze-before-you-sneeze).
- **Weeks 5 and 6, use it.** Sitting, then standing. 8-second holds.
- **Weeks 7 to 12, build it.** Holds up to 10 seconds, 3 sets a day. This is the stretch where the trial results live.
- **After week 12, keep it.** At least 3 days a week, the lowest dose published for the long run.<Ref n={5} />

## The real risk: stopping

In an app trial, 41% of women still trained every day at 3 months.<Ref n={6} />

The method works. The habit fades. So build the habit on purpose:

1. **Anchor it.** Same moments every day: coffee, lunch, brushing your teeth.
2. **Shrink it on hard days.** 1 set of 8 still counts.
3. **Track it.** A tick on a calendar, or a streak in the app.

In the app, one line sits next to your goal: "It's okay to suck, but it's not okay to skip."
