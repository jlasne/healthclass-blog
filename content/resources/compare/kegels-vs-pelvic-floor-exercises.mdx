## Are Kegels and pelvic floor exercises the same?

A Kegel is one pelvic floor exercise. Dr. Arnold Kegel, a gynecologist, described it in 1948.<Ref n={3} /> You squeeze and lift your pelvic floor, then let it go.

"Pelvic floor exercises" is the wider family. It holds slow holds, quick flicks, the Knack and letting go. A full routine adds the moves around them: breathing, posture, core and hips.

## What the research backs

Guidelines list pelvic floor training as the first step for stress and mixed leaks, for at least 3 months.<Ref n={1} />

A 2004 review weighed 3 ideas for why it works.<Ref n={2} /> Trials backed 2 of them:

- **You learn to squeeze before the push,** like the Knack before a cough.
- **Strength training builds the muscle,** so it supports you all day.

The third idea, that belly training strengthens the pelvic floor by itself, had no trial behind it. So core work adds to Kegels. It does not replace them.

A strong pelvic floor needs 5 ingredients: mobility, posture, core, gut and the squeeze. Kegels are proven to work. They are 1 piece of 5, and most women were never taught to do them right.<Ref n={5} />

## Which to start with

Start with the squeeze, done right. In a 1991 study, only 49% of women did a clean squeeze after a short spoken lesson.<Ref n={4} /> A check of your technique matters more than the number of reps.

Then add the other parts, one at a time. Stiff hips, a held breath or a hard stool can all make the squeeze harder.
