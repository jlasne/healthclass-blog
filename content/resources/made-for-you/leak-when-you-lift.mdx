## You are far from alone

In a 2019 survey of 134 women who compete in powerlifting, 37% leaked during training, meets or max lifts. In daily life, about 11% did.<Ref n={1} />

A 2025 review pooled 5 studies with 1,809 women. Leaks hit 41% to 49% of powerlifters and 37% to 54% of weightlifters. The deadlift came first, at 42.5%, then the squat, at 36.3%.<Ref n={2} />

A barbell is one lift among many. A toddler, a full laundry basket or a case of water asks for the same effort.

## Why a lift pushes urine out

To lift something heavy, your belly and back tighten. That pushes down on your bladder. Holding your breath pushes down even harder.

Your pelvic floor sits under your bladder and holds the exit closed. A heavy lift asks it to hold against a big push, rep after rep. Late in a set, it tires. In the review, heavy loads, high reps and meets were all linked to more leaks.<Ref n={2} />

## What helps

1. **Breathe out as you lift.** Breathe in to get ready. Breathe out through the hard part, like standing up from a squat. Your pelvic floor lifts as you breathe out.
2. **Squeeze just before the effort.** Lift your pelvic floor a split second before the weight moves, then hold through the lift. This timed squeeze is the Knack. Researchers tested it on coughs.<Ref n={3} /> Use the same timing for a lift.
3. **Find your dry load.** Lower the weight or the reps until you finish the set dry. Then add a little at a time.
4. **Train your pelvic floor every day.** Slow holds build the stamina a long set needs. Guidelines list at least 3 months of pelvic floor training as the first step for stress leaks.<Ref n={4} />

Powerlifters brace for a max lift by holding their breath. If you leak when you brace, lower the load for now and see a pelvic floor physical therapist who works with lifters.

## Try it today

Put a full grocery bag at your feet. Breathe in. As you breathe out, lift your pelvic floor, then pick up the bag and stand. Keep breathing out as you rise. Set it down and let go fully. Do it 5 times.
