## You are far from alone

In a US survey from 2015 to 2018, 61.8% of women said they had leaked in the past 12 months.<Ref n={2} /> A cough is one of the moments that can bring it on.

## Why a cough pushes urine out

A cough squeezes your belly hard and fast. That push lands on your bladder in a split second.

Your pelvic floor sits under your bladder like a hammock. When it lifts in time, it holds the exit closed. When it lifts late, or too softly for the push, a few drops get out.

## The squeeze that was tested on coughs

The Knack is a squeeze timed just before the push. Researchers first tested it on coughs.<Ref n={1} />

Women with mild to moderate stress leaks learned to squeeze before a cough. On a medium cough, leakage fell by 98.2% on average. On a deep cough, it fell by 73.3%.

The gap tells you something. A deep cough needs a stronger squeeze. That is what daily training builds.

## Through cold season

1. **Practice before the season.** Do 5 fake coughs a day with the Knack, sitting, then standing. Then every real cough becomes a rep.
2. **Train every day.** Slow holds build strength for deep coughs. Quick flicks build speed. Guidelines list at least 3 months of pelvic floor training as the first step for stress leaks.<Ref n={3} />
3. **Treat the cough too.** A cough that lasts more than 3 weeks needs a doctor's visit.<Ref n={4} /> Fewer coughs mean fewer pushes on your bladder.

## Try it today

Sit tall. Breathe in. As you breathe out, squeeze and lift as if you hold in gas and pee. Give a small cough while you hold. Let go fully. Rest 10 seconds. Do it 5 times.
