## Why the front door sets it off

Your bladder learns by repetition. Each time you rush to the toilet as you walk in, the link between home and peeing gets stronger. After a while, the key in the lock is enough to start the urge.

Researchers can see this link. In a 2020 study, photos of each woman's own trigger places changed bladder sensation in 11 of 12 women.<Ref n={1} /> In a 2022 study of 23 women, those photos lit up the brain's attention areas.<Ref n={2} />

## The arrival routine

1. **Keys away.** Put them in their bowl or on their hook.
2. **Shoes off.** Take your time.
3. **Bag on the hook.** Then walk calmly to the bathroom.

If 3 tasks feel too hard, start with 2. Add more as the urge eases and your bladder learns to wait its turn.

## Name your bladder

Give your bladder a name. When it calls at the door, tell it: "Not yet. I decide when." It sounds silly. It puts you back in charge, the way a dog learns a new routine through calm repetition.

## Try it tonight

As you walk to your door, breathe out slowly. If the urge comes, stand still and do 5 quick squeezes. Then do your 3 tasks, and walk, do not run, to the toilet.
