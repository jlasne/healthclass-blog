## What 8 hours at a desk do

**Slouching.** How you sit changes how your pelvic floor works. In a small study of 8 women, it worked at 7% of its full strength when they slumped. Sitting upright, it worked at 13%.<Ref n={1} />

**Tight chains.** Muscles link in chains, from your feet to your neck. After hours at a screen, your chest and hips get short and tight. That changes how your pelvis sits, and how your pelvic floor rests and lifts.<Ref n={3} />

**Clenching and holding it.** Stress makes you clench without noticing, in meetings or in traffic. Holding your pee and putting off a bowel movement can make your pelvic floor tense up.<Ref n={2} />

## What helps at your desk

1. **Sit tall, but easy.** Feet flat, weight on your sit bones, lower back supported. Tall, not stiff.
2. **Unclench every hour.** Breathe out slowly and let your jaw, belly and pelvic floor soften.
3. **Go when your body asks.** At work as at home. A meeting can wait 2 minutes.
4. **Stand up and move every hour.** Stretch the front of your hips, then open your chest in a doorway.

## Try it today, in your chair

Sit tall, feet flat. Breathe out and lift your pelvic floor for 5 seconds, as if you hold in gas. Let go for 5 seconds. Do 5. Then breathe slowly for 1 minute and let everything soften. No one at the next desk will notice.
