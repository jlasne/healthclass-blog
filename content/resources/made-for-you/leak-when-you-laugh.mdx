## You are far from alone

In a US survey from 2015 to 2018, 61.8% of women said they had leaked in the past 12 months.<Ref n={1} /> A laugh is one of the moments that can bring it on.

## Why a laugh is harder than a sneeze

A sneeze is one push. You can time one squeeze for it. A laugh is a string of pushes, and a long laugh can last 10 seconds or more.

So a laugh asks 2 things of your pelvic floor. **Speed**, to close at the first burst. **Stamina**, to stay closed until the last one. When it tires halfway, the leak comes late in the laugh.

## What helps

1. **Squeeze as the laugh starts.** Lift your pelvic floor at the first burst, like the Knack for a sneeze. In a small study, a squeeze before a medium cough cut leakage by 98%.<Ref n={3} />
2. **Keep a gentle hold.** Keep a light lift until the laugh ends, in place of a full squeeze for 10 seconds.
3. **Build stamina.** Slow holds of 5 to 10 seconds train the hold you need. Quick flicks train the start. Guidelines list at least 3 months of pelvic floor training as the first step for stress leaks.<Ref n={2} />
4. **Sit or stand tall.** Good posture helps your belly and your pelvic floor work as a team.

## When it is more than a few drops

Giggle incontinence is a rare condition. The whole bladder empties during a strong laugh, and it mostly starts in the teens.<Ref n={4} /> It needs a doctor, not exercises alone.

## Try it today

Sit tall. Lift your pelvic floor as if you hold in gas and pee. Keep a light hold while you count out loud, "ha, ha, ha", for 10 seconds. Let go fully. Rest 10 seconds. Do it 3 times.
