## What a school day does

In a survey of 791 teachers, they peed 2.7 times per work day on average. 24.5% went once or never. Half drank less at work on purpose, to avoid the toilet.<Ref n={1} />

Drinking less came at a cost. Teachers who drank less had 2.21 times the rate of bladder infections of those who drank what they wanted.<Ref n={1} />

## Why holding it matters

Holding your pee for hours keeps your pelvic floor on guard. Day after day, it can stay tense.

Cutting your water makes your urine darker and more concentrated. That irritates the bladder, so urges can get stronger, not weaker.

<Remember title="Dr. Hapee Bladder, on Instagram">

Go when your body signals, at work and at home. Holding urine and delaying bowel movements are direct causes of pelvic floor spasm, and health workers and teachers carry this risk through long shifts without breaks.<Ref n={2} />

## 5 habits that fit a school day

1. **Sip, do not cut.** Keep a bottle on your desk and sip between lessons. Aim for pale yellow urine.
2. **Use your breaks.** At recess and lunch, go if you feel even a mild need.
3. **Scale back caffeine.** Swap part of your coffee or tea for water, and keep the cup you love.
4. **Set up cover.** Agree with the teacher next door to watch your class for 3 minutes when you need it.
5. **Unclench between classes.** Breathe out slowly and let your jaw, belly and pelvic floor soften.

## Try it today

Fill a bottle before first period. Take 3 sips each time the bell rings. At lunch, check the color of your pee: pale yellow means you are on track.
