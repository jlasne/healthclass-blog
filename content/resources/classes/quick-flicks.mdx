## What a quick flick does

A quick flick is a Kegel you lift fast and let go fast. It trains speed: when a cough or a sneeze hits, your pelvic floor has a split second to close.

A 2004 review names 2 ways training helps with stress leaks. You learn to squeeze before the push, and your muscle grows stronger.<Ref n={2} /> Quick flicks train the first one: a fast squeeze, right on time.

Slow holds are its partner. They train stamina for a long laugh, a walk or a run. A good routine has both.

## How to do one

1. **Find your position.** Lie on your back, knees bent. It is the easiest place to start.
2. **Keep breathing.** A flick is too fast to match with each breath, so breathe normally through the set.
3. **Lift fast and hard** for 1 second, as if you hold in gas in a quiet room.
4. **Let go fully** for 1 to 3 seconds. Feel the muscle drop before the next one.
5. **Repeat 10 times.**

## Why speed matters

In a 1998 study, women with mild to moderate stress leaks learned to squeeze just before a cough. On a medium cough, leakage fell by 98.2% on average.<Ref n={3} />

That squeeze has to come fast, between the tickle in your throat and the cough. Quick flicks train that speed.

## How to build up

- **Count:** 5 flicks in a row, then 10.
- **Positions:** lying down, then sitting, then standing.
- **Rest:** 3 seconds between flicks at first, then 2, then 1.

## Common mistakes

- **Blurred flicks.** If the next flick starts before the last one ends, rest longer.
- **Squeezing your butt, thighs or belly.** Only the pelvic floor moves.
- **Holding your breath.** Keep breathing through the whole set.
