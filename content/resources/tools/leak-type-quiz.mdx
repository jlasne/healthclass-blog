## The 2 main patterns

**Stress leaks** happen with effort. A cough, a sneeze, a laugh, a jump or a heavy bag pushes on your bladder. If your pelvic floor closes late or too softly, a few drops get out.

**Urge leaks** come with a sudden, strong need to go. The urge arrives first. The leak follows, on the way to the toilet.

**Mixed leaks** are both. Among US women who leak, 37.5% have stress leaks only, 22% have urge leaks only and 31.3% have both.<Ref n={2} />

## How this quiz works

The 3 questions follow the shape of the 3 Incontinence Questions, a short test from a 2006 study of 301 women.<Ref n={1} />

In that study, the questions found 86% of the stress leaks and 75% of the urge leaks that a full doctor's evaluation found. That makes it a good first sort. It also means about 1 answer in 4 to 7 lands in the wrong group, so a doctor confirms.

## What to do with your result

- **Stress leaks:** guidelines list pelvic floor training for at least 3 months as the first step.<Ref n={3} /> A squeeze timed before a sneeze, called the Knack, helps on the spot.
- **Urge leaks:** see your doctor first. Bladder habits help many women: what you drink, when you go, and how you calm an urge.<Ref n={4} />
- **Mixed leaks:** start with the pattern that bothers you most. Your doctor can help you choose.

## Bring notes to your doctor

For 3 days, write down each leak: the time, what you were doing, and whether an urge came first. These notes help your doctor more than any quiz.
