## Why keep a bladder diary

The NICE guideline asks doctors to use a bladder diary when they first assess leaks or an overactive bladder. It asks for at least 3 days, covering a work day and a day off.<Ref n={1} />

Memory blurs. Was it 6 trips or 10? Did the leak come after a cough or after an urge? A diary shows the pattern in black and white.

## How to fill it in

1. **Print the 3 pages.** Keep them with a pen, near the toilet or in your bag.
2. **Write each drink:** what, and how much. A cup, a glass, a bottle.
3. **Tick each pee,** with the time.
4. **Write each leak:** S for drops, M for wet underwear, L for wet clothes. Add what you were doing.
5. **Rate each urge** from 0 to 3, even when no leak follows.
6. **Note when you woke up and went to bed,** so night trips show.

## What your diary can show

- **Effort or urge.** Leaks after a cough, a lift or a laugh point to stress leaks. Leaks after a strong urge point to urge leaks. The [leak type quiz](/resources/tools/leak-type-quiz) asks about the same pattern.
- **Night trips.** How many times you get up to pee.
- **Your drinks.** Too little, too much, or a lot of coffee, tea or soda.

Aim for balance: pale yellow urine. Too little and too much both make things worse. Drink first thing after waking, sip through the day, and stop 2 to 3 hours before bed. Scale back caffeine, and keep the drinks you love.<Ref n={2} />
