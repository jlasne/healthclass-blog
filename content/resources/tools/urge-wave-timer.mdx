## Why ride it out

An urge comes like a wave: it rises, peaks, then eases. Staying still gives the wave time to pass, instead of a rush to the toilet at its peak.

Each urge you ride out teaches your bladder to wait a little longer. That is the idea behind bladder training. The NICE guideline offers it for at least 6 weeks as the first step for urge leaks.<Ref n={2} />

## The 4 steps

1. **Stop.** Stay still. Sit down if you can.
2. **Squeeze.** 5 quick squeezes of your pelvic floor: lift fast, let go fully.
3. **Breathe.** In for 4, out for 6. Let your belly soften.
4. **Distract.** Count back from 100 by 3, or name 5 things you can see.

Then walk calmly to the toilet, or wait a few more minutes if you can.

## Does it work?

In a 1998 trial, 197 women aged 55 to 92 with urge leaks learned behavioral training, urge control included. Their leaks fell by 80.7% on average, against 39.4% with a placebo.<Ref n={1} />

## Track your urges

Note each strong urge in a [3-day bladder diary](/resources/tools/bladder-diary): the time, what you were doing, and how strong it felt. Over a few weeks, your diary shows whether they come less often.
