## What an overactive bladder is

The International Continence Society describes it as urgency, with or without urge leaks, usually with frequent trips and night trips.<Ref n={1} />

Urgency is the key: a sudden, strong need to pee that is hard to put off. A leak is not needed. Doctors call that "overactive bladder dry".

## How common it is

In a US survey of 5,204 adults, 16.9% of women had an overactive bladder. Urge leaks in women rose with age, from 2.0% to 19%, with a jump after 44.<Ref n={2} />

## What helps first

The NICE guideline offers bladder training for at least 6 weeks as the first step for urge leaks.<Ref n={3} /> You stretch the time between trips, a few minutes at a time. The [urge wave timer](/resources/tools/urge-wave-timer) walks you through riding out an urge.

<Remember title="Dr. Dana, on Instagram">

Aim for balance: pale yellow urine. Scale back caffeine, and keep the drinks you love. For the urge at the front door, do 3 small tasks before the toilet: keys away, shoes off, bag on the hook. Then walk calmly.<Ref n={4} />

</Remember>

## Before you see your doctor

Keep a [3-day bladder diary](/resources/tools/bladder-diary). It shows your trips by day and by night, your drinks, and what came before each urge. Your doctor reads your pattern from it.
