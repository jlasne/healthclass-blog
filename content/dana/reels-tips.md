# Women's Health Tips from Dr. Dana

Source: 50 reels from @dr_hapee_bladder, August 10 to November 8, 2026.
Figures and study results are as stated in the reels.
Take personal medical decisions to your own doctor.

## 1. Know your normal

1 in 2 women will leak urine.
Common and normal are two different things, so treat every symptom as fixable.
Women wait 6.5 years on average to raise bladder leaks, and fear of treatment is one of the top 3 reasons for that delay.
End that wait today.

- Use a handheld mirror to learn your normal.
- Get comfortable with your anatomy, because you spot changes first and early changes are easier to treat.
- Book the appointment even when you fear the treatment, since it costs one visit to learn your options and every condition has more than one option.
- Ask for help early.
- Expect specialist waits of 6 to 7 months, and start exercises, bladder training and constipation care right now.

## 2. Train your bladder

Your bladder learns by repetition.
Every rushed trip to the toilet teaches it to squeeze earlier next time.
Reverse that pattern with calm routines, and women with urgency, frequency and leaks can lower all 3 symptoms with patience and daily practice.
It takes repetition.

**The arrival routine**

- Do 3 tasks before the toilet when you get home.
- Try this order: keys away, shoes off, bag on the hook, then walk calmly to the bathroom.
- Start with 2 fast tasks if 3 feels too hard, then add more as your urgency drops and your bladder learns to wait its turn.
- Name your bladder.
- Talk to her as you pull into the driveway, and tell her each step before you walk in.
- A separate name turns urgency into a behavior you coach, the same way a dog learns a bell means food, and your bladder unlearns it through repeated calm arrivals.

**Calm your nervous system**

- Stress revs up your bladder.
- A nervous system stuck in fight or flight raises urgency and frequency, and leaks can follow.
- Slow down on purpose during the day, and release your pelvic floor each time you catch yourself clenching it for hours at your desk or in traffic.
- Unclench every hour.

**Use toilets on time**

- Go when your body signals, at work and at home.
- Holding urine and delaying bowel movements are direct causes of pelvic floor spasm, and health workers and teachers carry this risk through long shifts without breaks.
- Carry disposable seat covers in your purse.

## 3. Drink to pee less

Drink more to pee less.
Concentrated urine irritates the bladder lining and triggers more spasms and more trips.
Water dilutes your urine, lowers that irritation, and lets your bladder hold more volume each time, so cutting your fluids makes overactive bladder symptoms worse.
Keep drinking water.

- Sip steadily and evenly through the day.
- Chugging water at intervals overloads a bladder that already has a smaller capacity.
- Stay within reason, because 2 gallons of water in a day will send you to the toilet again and again from morning to night.
- Cut coffee, tea, soda and alcohol.
- Replace part of those drinks with water to lower urgency and frequency.
- Cut back fluids 1 to 2 hours before bedtime to reduce nighttime trips.

## 4. Treat bladder leaks

2 main types exist.
Your treatment plan depends on which type you have, and you can have both.
Stress leaks come with coughs, sneezes, jumps, laughs and lifts, and physical therapy, strengthening, injections or surgery give them a high cure rate.
Urge leaks work differently.

- Urge leaks come with rushing, frequency and waking at night to pee.
- Bladder training and physical therapy reverse them for a share of women, and the rest stay on ongoing care such as medication or repeat Botox injections.
- Ask your doctor for one plan covering both leak types.
- A bladder lift treats vaginal prolapse.
- Urge leaks are a function problem, so a lift corrects your anatomy with no promise of curing leaks.
- Consider a prolapse repair only for prolapse symptoms.
- Core strength, pelvic floor exercises, constipation care and weight loss all improve leaks, so complete these before you reach a specialist.

**Lichen sclerosus and leaks**

- Treat leaks early with this condition.
- Lichen sclerosus with incontinence carries a higher vulvar cancer risk than lichen sclerosus alone, because urine is a waste product that irritates skin.
- Use clobetasol twice a week as maintenance, including on days you feel fine, and leave flare-only use behind.

**Plan your medication costs**

- Check your plan during open enrollment.
- Confirm that bladder medications such as Gemtesa or Myrbetriq stay covered next year, since January brings a wave of coverage changes and forced switches.
- See your doctor before enrollment closes if your symptoms are uncontrolled, so you can compare plans against the exact drug your doctor plans to prescribe.

## 5. Catch dangerous symptoms

5 symptoms need a urologist.
Women die from bladder cancer at a higher rate than men, and delayed diagnosis is a likely reason.

1. Urgency that borders on pain.
2. Pain when you pee, especially at the end of your stream, because this pattern points beyond a simple infection and needs a full workup by a specialist.
3. Repeated UTI symptoms with negative cultures.
4. A sudden change in urgency and frequency that appears abruptly and stays.
5. Blood in your urine, whether you see it or a test finds it, and a negative culture means you see a urologist.

- Track every episode in one place.
- Urgent care visits and antibiotics across different clinics hide the pattern, so bring your full history and all your culture results to one urologist.
- See one doctor consistently.

**Recurrent UTIs**

- 2 UTIs in 6 months counts as recurrent, and so do 3 in a year.
- National guidelines call for a full evaluation at that point, including a pelvic exam, so ask for one by name at your next visit.
- Ask about vaginal estrogen.

**Protect an aging parent**

- Ask whether she uses a vaginal pessary.
- A pessary left in for years can erode into the vagina, bladder, rectum or pelvic cavity, and the risk peaks with dementia and new doctors.
- Require a pelvic exam for back-to-back UTIs.

## 6. Build the full pelvic floor recipe

Kegels are 1 ingredient of 5.
Each ingredient below supports the next, so build them in this order.
Women who rely on Kegels alone often report zero change, and on exam nearly all of them turn out to be contracting incorrectly with a weak pelvic floor.
Use all 5.

1. Mobility: stretch, build body awareness, reduce stress and practice diaphragmatic breathing.
2. Posture: global postural exercises improve pelvic floor function and lower your disorder risk in studies.
3. Core: planks, bird dogs, dead bugs and boat pose build the stability your pelvic floor needs during every lift, cough and step you take.
4. Gut: eat fiber and drink water with it.
5. Kegels: add these last, after the first 4 are in place, and do them with the technique below.

**How to do a Kegel**

- Lift as if you were stopping your urine stream or holding in gas, or picture holding a marble inside your vagina.
- Do sustained holds first.
- Start with 3 seconds on and 3 seconds off, then build to 5 and later 10 seconds once the shorter hold feels easy.
- Add quick flicks next.
- Squeeze as hard as you can for 1 second, then rest for 1 second.
- Sample routine: 10 sustained holds and 10 quick flicks, done twice a day while you brush your teeth, morning and evening.
- Do them off the toilet.

**Fix the 3 common mistakes**

1. Isolate the pelvic floor, and keep your butt and inner thighs relaxed throughout.
2. Breathe through every contraction.
3. Relax fully between reps, because a muscle needs to lengthen completely before it can shorten with full strength and a full range of motion.

- See a pelvic floor physical therapist if you struggle to find the right muscles.

## 7. Release a tight pelvic floor

1 in 10 people have it.
High-tone pelvic floor means muscles stay in spasm, and Kegels make it worse.
Symptoms include urgency, incomplete emptying, bladder pain, constipation, painful sex and pelvic pain, and risk factors include chronic stress, past abuse, endometriosis, surgical scars and IBS.
Tightness equals weakness.

**Check yourself**

- Feel for tender spots and uneven muscles.
- Contract, then relax on purpose, and look for a clear movement in both directions.
- Breathe out and feel the muscle soften and drop into your hand, because a muscle that stays still on exhale points to high tone.

**Correct it**

- Pause Kegels for now.
- Lengthen and relax first, with targeted stretches, diaphragmatic breathing, stress reduction and body awareness, and most women need nothing beyond consistent practice of these 4.
- Strengthen after the tension resolves.

**If you need more**

- Use a pelvic wand on trigger points.
- Sit on a 3 to 4 inch massage ball, which acts as a foam roller down there.
- See a pelvic floor physical therapist and a urogynecologist, who can offer trigger point injections, pelvic floor Botox or muscle relaxant suppositories when home care stops helping you.

## 8. Lift weights safely

Zero exercises are proven harmful.
Weight training raises bone density, improves glucose control and lowers dementia risk.
Muscle loss raises your pelvic floor disorder risk, so building muscle protects you, on the condition that you manage where the pressure of each lift goes.
Pressure decides the outcome.

**Learn 360 degree breathing**

- Picture a balloon inside your torso.
- Inhale and expand your ribs outward, upward, forward and backward.
- Your pelvic floor drops as you inhale and lifts as you exhale, in step with your diaphragm, and tight muscles elsewhere push that force down onto it.
- Keep your exhale passive.

**Find your restrictions**

- Wrap your hands around your ribcage.
- Breathe deeply and watch your fingertips move apart while your belly stays soft.
- Get on all fours and use a mirror to confirm that your back rises and falls with every full breath you take.
- Target each restriction you find.
- Fix them at rest with postural stretches, then carry that breathing into squats, lunges and lifts.
- Start with low weight and progress slowly.
- Get help if you cannot identify your restrictions.

## 9. Strengthen your deep core

The core is the glue.
Your deep corset muscle fires a split second before you move.
It stabilizes your back and pelvis and holds your organs in place, and she has yet to meet a prolapse patient with a strong core.
Train it directly.

1. Hold forearm planks.
2. Dead bugs, starting with toe taps and adding weight as you get stronger.
3. Bird dogs, which also build strength in your shoulders and glutes.
4. Stomach vacuums: exhale, draw in the corset muscle, hold, release, first lying down, then on all fours, then seated or standing.
5. Add daily draw-ins.

- Hold a draw-in at your desk, at traffic lights or during TV, while staying able to talk.
- Spread these across your week, and pair them with stretching and breathing so your muscles keep their full range of motion.
- Release between sessions.

## 10. Protect muscle and posture

Grip strength predicts your risk.
A 5% drop in grip strength over time significantly raises your risk of leaks and prolapse.
Adults lose 25% of peak muscle mass between ages 40 and 70, then 2% each year after 70 without deliberate training to maintain that muscle.
Measure yours at home.

- Buy a low-cost hand dynamometer online.
- Squeeze as hard as you can for under 3 seconds, then alternate hands and rest 30 seconds.
- Record your best of 3.
- Below 16 kg meets the threshold for muscle loss in women, and your device can show the normal range for your age and sex.
- Retest over time, and answer a weak score with full-body strength training along with hand exercises, since grip reflects your whole body.
- Fix your posture daily.
- If you sit for hours, use a chair with lower back and pelvic support that controls pelvic tilt.

## 11. Fix your gut

Constipation strains your pelvic floor.
What goes in your mouth shapes what comes out and how your bladder feels.
Eat fiber, drink plenty of water with that fiber, and work on your gut microbiome and motility, because constipation and prolapse can feed each other over time.
Treat constipation early.

## 12. Use vaginal estrogen

Women in their 20s benefit too.
Vaginal estrogen significantly lowers the risk of UTIs, along with sepsis and death from UTIs.
It also reduces leaks, prolapse symptoms, pelvic pain, painful sex, and the burning and urgency of genitourinary symptoms of menopause, with a local effect on tissue.
Ask your doctor.

**When to start**

- Start at 40 or later for prevention.
- Start earlier if perimenopause symptoms arrive before age 40, even in your 30s.
- Start at any adult age for recurrent UTIs, and women can begin for the first time even at 90 and still gain from it.

**Who can use it**

- You can add it to a patch, pill or hormone therapy, because systemic estrogen reaches vulvar tissue less well and the local dose adds no meaningful blood level.
- Women with endometriosis, fibroids, stroke history, clotting disorders or smoking can use it.
- Cancer history needs a personal discussion.
- Ovarian, uterine and breast cancer survivors can use it after a risk review, and fewer than 5% of breast cancer survivors get offered it today.
- Women on tamoxifen or aromatase inhibitors such as anastrozole need a risk-benefit talk with severe symptoms, since studies on this group are still in progress.

**How to apply the cream**

1. Attach the applicator and fill it to your prescribed dose.
2. Or use your finger, measuring cream to the first knuckle.
3. Insert it like a tampon and rub it around inside, the same way you rub cream into your face, which also reduces mess.
4. Add a thin coat outside.
5. Cover the vulva from clitoris to the back, including the inner labia and the urethral opening, especially if you get UTIs.

- Options include cream, capsule and ring.
- Tell your doctor: "I want vaginal estrogen to prevent age-related changes" or "to prevent UTIs," and ask for the specific risk if the answer is no.
- Compare low-cost pharmacies such as Mark Cuban's Cost Plus, where it can cost $13.

**Get more from an estrogen patch**

- Place it on your upper outer buttock.
- That spot absorbs more than your abdomen, and rotating sites regularly prevents skin irritation.
- Alternate between buttock and abdomen so the location tells you whether you already changed your patch.
- Apply it to fully dry skin.
- Keep lotion and sunscreen away from the site, press firmly for 30 seconds, and reinforce lifting edges with a Tegaderm film.
- Remove leftover adhesive with mineral or olive oil.
- Ask for a gel, spray or mist if the patch keeps failing you after you have tried each of these fixes.

## 13. Decide about prolapse

2 reasons justify surgery.
The first is bother: discomfort, low confidence or constant worry all count.
The second is a health risk, such as kidney blockage, 200 to 400 cc of urine left after you pee, repeat infections, or skin breakdown after a pessary fails.
Your reason is valid.

- Know the signs: a bulge when you wipe, or a feeling of sitting on a ball.
- Choose surgery for your own goals, and leave an exam finding you cannot feel out of that decision.
- Get a yearly kidney ultrasound with grade 3 or 4 prolapse if you choose to wait, because kidney blockage from a kinked ureter can build slowly with zero symptoms.
- Try standard UTI prevention before surgery for infections.
- Consider a pessary as a non-surgical option.

**Pessary care**

- Remove and wash it on the schedule your doctor sets.
- Ask your doctor for vaginal estrogen, which lowers your risk of ulcers in the vaginal walls.
- Set a recurring calendar alert every 3 months labeled "pessary check," so that anyone who takes over your care can see the device exists and act on it.

## 14. Prepare for surgery

Ask 4 questions before a hysterectomy.
Each one protects a specific part of your pelvic health after surgery.

1. Will you support the top of the vagina during surgery, since removing the uterus cuts ligaments and slightly raises your prolapse risk?
2. Which approach best preserves vaginal length?
3. Should my fallopian tubes come out, even if my ovaries stay in place?
4. Will you prescribe pelvic floor physical therapy for my recovery, the same way joint replacement patients get rehab after their operations?

**Remove your tubes**

- Removing them can cut ovarian cancer risk by up to 80%.
- Most ovarian cancer starts in the fallopian tubes, and it has no universal screening test.
- Ask about removal during a hysterectomy or a final C-section, and studies found no change in hormones or menopausal symptoms from removing tubes alone.
- It adds no significant operating time.

**Demand a full risk discussion**

Expect 4 parts from your surgeon.

1. The published risk statistics for your procedure, including recurrence rates.
2. Your personal risk, based on history and conditions such as connective tissue disorders or mast cell activation.
3. A specific plan for each risk, so you know how your team responds if that complication happens during or after your procedure.
4. The alternatives, including what happens if you skip surgery entirely.

- Take your time.
- Get a second opinion, book a follow-up visit, and build trust with your surgeon before you commit to a date on the calendar.
- Change doctors if trust is missing.

**Choose your mindset**

- Replace "I'm always the 1%" with "I'll do well" once you have decided and trust your plan.
- Optimism correlates with better recovery from surgery, a 30% lower risk of heart attack, stroke and cardiovascular death, and a 5 to 15% longer life.
- Nerves are normal before surgery.
- Practice optimism as a skill by naming 5 things you are grateful for every night before sleep.

## 15. Protect your heart

Heart disease kills women most.
80% of stroke victims have zero symptoms before their first stroke.
Women are 50% more likely to be misdiagnosed during a cardiac event and 2.5 times less likely to reach a cardiologist, and their risk climbs sharply around age 60.
Know your baseline.

- Learn the wider symptom picture for women, and speak up when your symptoms differ from chest pain and left arm pain.
- Consider vascular screening from age 40, and earlier if your family history includes cardiovascular disease.
- Ask about carotid artery, abdominal aortic aneurysm, peripheral vascular and atrial fibrillation checks, since these 4 non-invasive tests can fit in one 60 minute appointment.
- Add a bone density test.
- Repeat screening over time, especially as you approach and reach menopause.

## 16. Invest in your life

Your people shape your final years.
Ask who would care for you at the end, and give them your time.
Stop measuring yourself against neighbors, and put that energy into your spouse, your closest friends and the people who would show up on your hardest day.
Keep up with them.

**Before you ask for a raise**

- Research from Seton Hall links subtle makeup to higher trust and more money.
- Use it as a tool if you choose, and ask for that promotion, since women ask less often.
- Her routine takes under 5 minutes: tinted sunscreen, blush kept outside the corner of your eye, bronzer above it, then mascara and a small line of eyeliner.
