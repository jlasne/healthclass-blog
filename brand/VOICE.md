# Voice: how we write

For every word: app, website, letters, store, social, emails, replies. The founder's 7 copy rules come first.

## The 7 copy rules

1. **No em dashes.** Use a period, a comma or a colon.
2. **Sentences under 30 words.**
3. **Data instead of adjectives.** "56% reported cure" beats "very effective".
4. **No weasel words.** Cut "may", "can help", "many experts say" unless the source says it. Say who and how many.
5. **The "so what" test.** If a line does not change what she does or believes, cut it.
6. **The simplest wording.** The short word. The plain verb.
7. **Say what she gets, not what we do not do.** Avoid negations unless the outcome is the negation ("Laugh without worrying").

## Replies and summaries (for chats with the founder)

tl;dr first. Only the decision and the key parts. Simple, organized words. No code in the answer unless asked.

## The claims rule (we broke it twice, never again)

- Every number has a source (Cochrane, NICE, ACOG, a named study). The source goes on the website page. The email only links to it.
- Never invent a fact, a quote, an experience or a product detail. Two unsupported lines were caught in review and removed. Check each claim against its source before it ships.
- No "cure" as a promise, no diagnosis, no treatment promise, no "best", "#1", "top", no ranking.
- Medical content is Dr. Kivlin's. If we are unsure, we ask her.
- No price, "free" or trial length in store images.
- A line about someone's real experience is written only if it is true for the person who posts it.

## Tone

Warm, direct, a knowledgeable friend. A urologist's authority without jargon. First person for Dr. Kivlin's letters ("I").
"We" for the team. "You" for her. Short paragraphs. Numbers over adjectives.

## Words

| Use | Avoid |
|---|---|
| leak, leaks, bladder leaks | incontinence (once, as the search word, then "leaks") |
| pelvic floor | "core" for the pelvic floor |
| squeeze, let go | "contract", "relax" in the cues |
| class, program, week | "course", "module" |
| Class Notes (the newsletter) | "blog newsletter", "mailing list" |
| letters (the articles) | "posts", "blog posts" |
| women | "ladies" |
| the app opens soon | "coming soon" in a headline, "waitlist" |

## Patterns that work

- **Subject lines:** 2 to 7 words, under 60 characters. Preview: one number or one promise, under 110 characters.
- **Letter body:** under 350 words. `Hi {{first_name|there}},` then a hook, the idea with a source mark like [1], a bold "Try it today:" step, a bold "A question I hear in clinic" answered in 3 to 5 lines, a bold "See a doctor if" only when the topic has warning signs, one main link, the app line once, the sign-off. Bold lines, never `#` headings.
- **Headlines:** the moment she gets back. "Laugh out loud again." "Sneeze without crossing your legs." The key word in italics.
- **Buttons:** what happens. "Get the app", "Get the launch-day email".
- **Errors:** what went wrong and how to fix it. No apology.

## Examples

| Not this | This |
|---|---|
| Our revolutionary app dramatically improves bladder control. | In a Cochrane review, 56% of women who trained reported cure of stress leaks, against 6% without. |
| We don't want you to worry about leaks. | Laugh without worrying. |
| Kegels can really help many women. | Half of us squeeze the wrong way at first. This letter shows the right one. |
| Click here to sign up for our exciting newsletter! | Get the launch-day email. |
