Hi {{first_name|there}},

This one is for readers who are pregnant, or planning to be.

Pregnancy puts 9 months of growing load on your pelvic floor. Then birth stretches it. You can train for that, the way you would train for a long hike.

A Cochrane review pooled 11 trials and 3,307 pregnant women [1]. Women who did pelvic floor training during pregnancy were 22% less likely to report leaks in late pregnancy. The authors rate the evidence as moderate quality.

Timing matters. The same review found weaker results for blanket training programs offered to every woman after birth [1]. So my advice: start during pregnancy.

**Check with your doctor first if**

- You have bleeding, placenta problems or a risk of preterm labor.
- You had pelvic, belly or vaginal surgery in the last 3 months.
- You have pelvic pain, or pain with sex.
- You see or feel a bulge at the vaginal opening.

The basics match any Kegel: lift on the out-breath, as if holding in gas. Let go fully on the in-breath. 8 lifts, 3 times a day [2].

What changes with the bump:

- **Positions.** As your belly grows, train sitting, lying on your side, or on hands and knees.
- **The let-go.** It matters even more now. For a vaginal birth, your pelvic floor needs to soften and stretch.

**Try it today: side-lying lifts**

Lie on your left side, a pillow between your knees. Breathe out and lift for 3 seconds. Breathe in and let go for 6 seconds. 8 reps, twice a day.

**A question I hear in clinic**

"When can I start again after the birth?"

When your doctor clears you. If you gave birth in the last 6 weeks, or had a cesarean, check with your doctor before you train. Then start gently, with the let-go first, and rebuild the lift over the following weeks.
