Hi {{first_name|there}},

"Stop your pee midstream. That is your Kegel."

You have read it in magazines. As a urologist, I would like to retire it.

Peeing is teamwork: your bladder squeezes while your pelvic floor relaxes to open the exit. Stop midstream, and you slam the exit shut while your bladder still squeezes.

Why I would skip it:

- **It may leave urine behind.** A clinical trial record on this exact exercise notes that no guideline recommends it [1].
- **It trains the wrong reflex.** Your pelvic floor learns to tighten when it should let go.
- **It is the wrong dose.** Guidelines ask for 8 lifts, 3 times a day, for 3 months [2].

3 better ways to find the muscle:

1. **The gas trick.** Imagine holding in gas. Gently lift that spot.
2. **The breath.** Lie down. Breathe out slowly and lift. Breathe in and let it soften.
3. **The finger check.** With a clean finger just inside your vagina, squeeze. You should feel a light hug and a pull upward.

**Try it today: 3 bathroom habits**

1. Sit all the way down. Hovering over the seat keeps your pelvic floor tense.
2. Lean forward a little, elbows on your knees, feet flat or on a small stool.
3. Take your time, and let the stream finish without pushing.

**A question I hear in clinic**

"Can I stop my pee once, just to feel the muscle?"

Some clinicians use a single stop to help a woman locate the muscle. If you try it, do it once, then let the stream finish fully. Keep your daily practice off the toilet.

**See a doctor if**

- You struggle to start peeing, or your stream is slow or stops and starts on its own.
- Your bladder still feels full after you go.
- It burns when you pee, or you see blood in your urine.
