Hi {{first_name|there}},

25 to 35 grams of fiber a day. That is the goal in the guide I give every patient [1].

Here is 1 day from my food chart that reaches 25.5 grams:

- **Breakfast:** 1 cup of cooked oatmeal (3 g) with ½ cup of raspberries (8 g).
- **Lunch:** ½ cup of cooked lentils (6.6 g).
- **Snack:** 1 medium pear (5.5 g).
- **Dinner:** ½ cup of broccoli (2.4 g).

That day mixes both kinds of fiber. Soluble fiber is the softener: it forms a gel and keeps moisture in your stool. Insoluble fiber is the bulker: it bulks up stool and stretches the bowel to trigger movement [1].

Why does a urologist care? Less constipation means a lower risk of pelvic floor problems, like bladder leaks and vaginal prolapse [1].

4 steps I go over with my patients:

1. **Eat both kinds.** Together they give the best bowel movements.
2. **Raise your water as you add fiber.** Water is the activator. Aim for balance: pale yellow urine. Without enough, constipation and bloating can get worse.
3. **Go slow.** Add only a few grams a week. Your body adjusts and bloating stays low.
4. **Food first.** The best health data is on fiber from food. Supplements alone are not enough.

Far from 25 today? Climb there over a few weeks.

**Try it today: count your grams**

1. Write down what you eat today.
2. Look up each food in my chart and add the grams.
3. Pick 1 food to add next week, with 1 extra glass of water.

**A question I hear in clinic**

"Which foods help constipation the most?"

My short list: raspberries and blackberries, kiwi, oatmeal, and pears, apples and prunes. Prunes add sorbitol, a natural laxative. Then chia and flax seeds, beans and legumes, and greens like broccoli and Brussels sprouts. If you start anywhere, start there.
