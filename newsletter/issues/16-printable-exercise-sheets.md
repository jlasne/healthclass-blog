Some of us like a sheet of paper more than any screen.

If that is you, here is a good find. The National Association For Continence (NAFC) is a US non-profit for bladder and bowel health. It offers free downloadable pelvic floor exercise sheets you can print.

A sheet works best with a plan around it. Here is what I would tape next to it, from Class Notes so far:

- **Lift on the out-breath.** Let go on the in-breath. A held breath pushes down.
- **Let go for twice as long as you lift,** while you learn. The let-go is half the exercise.
- **At least 8 lifts, 3 times a day, for at least 3 months.** That is the floor the UK guideline (NICE) sets [1].
- **3 anchors.** Same moments every day: coffee, lunch, brushing your teeth.
- **A calendar.** Tick each day you train. On a hard day, 1 set of 8 still counts.

**Try it today: 1 set at the fridge**

Stand tall at your fridge. Breathe out and lift for 3 seconds, as if you hold in gas. Breathe in and let go for 6 seconds. Do 8. If standing still feels hard, sit down and do them there.

**A question I hear in clinic**

"Do I need a device to do this right?"

No. A correct Kegel takes 2 seconds and 0 equipment. What helps most is a check of your technique. Guidelines ask a clinician to confirm your squeeze with a gentle internal exam before you train [1]. A pelvic floor physical therapist can feel what your muscle does.
