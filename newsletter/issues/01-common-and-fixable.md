Hi {{first_name|there}},

6 in 10 American women leaked urine in the past year [1].

That number comes from a national survey of 5,006 women.

Here is the part to keep: common and normal are two different words. Gray hair is normal. A leak when you laugh is a signal. A muscle is asking for training.

And muscles answer to training.

Of US women who leak [1]:

- **37.5% have stress leaks.** A laugh, a sneeze, a jump, a heavy bag.
- **31.3% have mixed leaks.** Both kinds.
- **22% have urgency leaks.** A sudden "I have to go now," often on the way to the bathroom.

Your type decides your first step. So find it first.

For stress and mixed leaks, I recommend pelvic floor muscle training first. So do NICE in the UK and ACOG in the US [2] [3]. In a Cochrane review, 56% of women with stress leaks who trained reported cure. Without training, 6% did [4].

It costs nothing and takes 5 minutes a day, on your couch.

**Try it today: the 1-week leak log**

For 7 days, write each leak in 2 words: what you were doing, and when. "Laughing, 3 pm." "Rushing to the bathroom, 7 am." After a week, your type is clear. Your doctor gets a head start too.

**A question I hear in clinic**

"How long until I notice a difference?"

Plan for 12 weeks of practice. That is the minimum the guidelines set [2]. My next letter shows you where your pelvic floor is.

**See a doctor if**

- You see blood in your urine, feel burning when you pee, or have a fever.
- You see or feel a bulge at the vaginal opening.
- You struggle to start peeing, or your bladder still feels full after you go.
- Your leaks started after surgery, radiation or a new medicine.
