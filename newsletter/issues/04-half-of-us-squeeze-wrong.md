Hi {{first_name|there}},

Here is a study I think about a lot.

Researchers gave 47 women brief spoken instructions for a Kegel. Then they checked each woman's squeeze [1].

23 did it right: 49%. 12 pushed down instead of lifting, a squeeze that can make leaks worse.

If a Kegel feels confusing, you are in good company. It is a skill. Here is how I teach it.

**Find the right muscle.** Imagine holding in gas in a quiet room. Gently squeeze and lift that spot, inside. Your buttocks, thighs and belly stay soft.

**Use my 5 cues.**

1. "Lift, like holding in gas."
2. "Breathe out as you lift." A held breath pushes down.
3. "Small and inside."
4. "Buttocks soft, thighs soft."
5. "Now let it all go."

**Check with a mirror.** On a good lift, the skin between your vagina and anus draws in and up. If it bulges out, you are pushing: lighten the effort and think "up and in."

**Try it today: your first 2 sets**

Lie on your back, knees bent. Lift for 2 seconds on an out-breath. Let go for 4 seconds on an in-breath. Do 5. Rest 20 seconds. Do 5 more. Half effort is plenty.

**A question I hear in clinic**

"How many Kegels should I do a day?"

UK guidelines (NICE) set at least 8 contractions, 3 times a day, for at least 3 months [2]. They also ask a clinician to confirm your squeeze with a gentle internal exam before you train. A pelvic floor physical therapist can feel what your muscle does.

**See a doctor or a pelvic floor physical therapist if**

- The lift still feels unclear after 2 weeks of daily practice.
- You feel pain when you squeeze.
- You feel heaviness or a bulge at the vaginal opening.
