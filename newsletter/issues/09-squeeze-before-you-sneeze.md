Hi {{first_name|there}},

Stress leaks have triggers you can feel coming. A sneeze. A cough. A laugh. A heavy box.

That gives you about 1 second of warning. The Knack puts that second to work.

**The Knack: squeeze and lift your pelvic floor just before the pressure hits. Hold through the sneeze. Then let go.**

Researchers taught a group of older women with mild to moderate stress leaks to squeeze before a cough. Then they measured the leak [1]:

- **98.2% less** leakage on a medium cough.
- **73.3% less** on a deep cough.

Here is the fine print. This was 1 small study of selected women. A larger randomized trial was designed in 2021 to test the Knack on its own [2].

I teach it anyway. It costs nothing, takes 1 second, and builds a reflex you keep for life.

A reflex needs reps tied to moments you already have. Pair the Knack with 3 cues:

1. Before you stand up.
2. Before you lift anything heavier than a bag of groceries.
3. The moment you feel a sneeze tickle.

The Knack works best on a pelvic floor that can lift fast. So keep up your daily lifts too.

**Try it today: 5 Knack coughs**

Stand tall. Breathe in. Squeeze and lift your pelvic floor. Keep the lift and cough once. Let go fully. Rest 5 seconds. Repeat 5 times.

**A question I hear in clinic**

"How long does it take to learn the Knack?"

Most of the practice fits in 1 week. Days 1 and 2: fake coughs. Days 3 and 4: squeeze first, then rise from your chair. Days 5 and 6: squeeze before you pick up a bag or a child. From day 7, every real sneeze and laugh is a rep.

Next sneeze, you have 1 second. What will you do with it?
