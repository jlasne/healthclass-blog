Hi {{first_name|there}},

Picture a hammock.

It stretches from your pubic bone in front to your tailbone in back. Your bladder, your uterus and your bowel rest on it.

That hammock is your pelvic floor, at the base of your pelvis. It has 3 jobs.

**1. Hold everything up.** When you stand, cough or lift a toddler, the pressure in your belly rises. The hammock takes that load.

**2. Keep the exits closed, then open them.** The urethra, the vagina and the anus pass through it. The muscles squeeze to keep things in and relax to let things out. The International Continence Society counts relaxing as part of normal function, right next to squeezing [1].

**3. Sex.** The same muscles play a part in sensation and orgasm. They also need to relax for comfortable sex.

It moves with your breath. Breathe in, and your diaphragm and your pelvic floor both move down. Breathe out, and both move up [2]. That comes from MRI scans of 8 healthy women: a useful picture from a small study. It is why I teach the lift on the out-breath.

**Try it today: find your hammock in 60 seconds**

1. Lie on your back, knees bent, feet flat.
2. Breathe in for 4 seconds. Let your belly and your pelvic floor soften.
3. Breathe out for 6 seconds and gently lift, as if holding in gas.
4. Let it all go on the next in-breath.

Do this 5 times at half effort.

**A question I hear in clinic**

"How do I know I am squeezing the right muscle?"

You feel a gentle squeeze and lift inside, like holding in gas. Your buttocks, thighs and belly stay soft, and you keep breathing. If the lift still feels unclear after 2 weeks of practice, see a pelvic floor physical therapist.

Training starts with knowing where the muscle is. Now you do.
