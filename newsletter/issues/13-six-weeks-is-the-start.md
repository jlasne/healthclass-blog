Hi {{first_name|there}},

"How long until it works?"

Here is the answer from the research: plan on 12 weeks, and feel changes along the way.

The trials that show pelvic floor training works ran 3 to 6 months [1]. So NICE asks for at least 3 months of training as the first treatment for stress or mixed leaks [2].

**The daily dose**

- At least 8 contractions, 3 times a day: the NICE floor [2].
- About 24 a day, for 6 weeks or more. In a meta-analysis, benefits stopped growing past that dose [3].

**What to expect, stage by stage**

- **Weeks 1 and 2, find it.** The lift becomes clear. Holds of 2 to 3 seconds.
- **Weeks 3 and 4, hold it.** 5-second holds. Add the Knack.
- **Weeks 5 and 6, use it.** Sitting, then standing. 8-second holds.
- **Weeks 7 to 12, build it.** Holds up to 10 seconds, 3 sets a day.

The real risk is stopping. In an app trial, 41% of women still trained every day at 3 months [4]. So build the habit on purpose:

1. **Anchor it.** Same moments every day: coffee, lunch, brushing your teeth.
2. **Shrink it on hard days.** 1 set of 8 still counts.
3. **Track it.** A tick on a calendar is enough.

**Try it today: set your 3 anchors**

Pick 3 moments you already have every day. Do 8 lifts at each one. Write the 3 moments on a sticky note and put it on your bathroom mirror.

**A question I hear in clinic**

"Do I have to do Kegels forever?"

To keep the results, yes, but less often. After 12 weeks, a routine of at least 3 days a week is the lowest dose published for maintenance [5]. Think of it as 6 weeks to learn, 6 more to build, then a lighter routine for life.
