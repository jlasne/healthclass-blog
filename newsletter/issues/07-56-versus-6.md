If you remember 1 number from my letters, make it this one: 56%.

In a Cochrane review, 56% of women with stress leaks who did pelvic floor muscle training reported cure. In the groups without training, 6% did [1].

A Cochrane review pools the best trials on one question. This number comes from 4 trials and 165 women, after 3 to 6 months of training [1].

Cure is the high bar. Count the women who were cured or improved, and it was 74% with training against 11% without (3 trials, 242 women). Women who trained also had about 1 fewer leak every 24 hours (7 trials, 432 women) [1].

That is why every guideline puts it first:

- **NICE, UK:** at least 3 months of supervised training, first, for stress or mixed leaks [2].
- **ACOG, US:** effective first-line care for stress, urgency and mixed leaks [3].
- **AUA, US:** pelvic floor exercises are a non-surgical option [4].

First-line means: start here, before pills or procedures.

One catch. In a Swedish trial of 123 women, 55.7% of those given a training app rated themselves much improved, versus 5% without it. Yet at 3 months, 41% of app users still trained every day [5]. The method works. The habit is the hard part.

**Try it today: today's 3 sets**

8 lifts of 3 seconds, each followed by 6 seconds of let-go. Once in the morning, once at lunch, once in the evening. That is the NICE floor: 8 contractions, 3 times a day [2].

US guidelines (ACOG) call pelvic floor training effective first-line care for stress, urgency and mixed leaks [3]. The strongest cure numbers come from women with stress leaks. Urgency leaks have their own plan, so build it with your doctor.
