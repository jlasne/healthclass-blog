Why does a urologist hand out a fiber guide?

Because your bladder and your bowel rest on the same hammock. Less constipation means a lower risk of pelvic floor problems: bladder leaks, vaginal prolapse and urinary retention [1].

Here is the short version of the guide I give every patient.

**Your goal: 25 to 35 grams of fiber a day, from food first [1].**

You need 2 kinds of fiber, plus water:

- **Soluble fiber, the softener.** It dissolves in water and forms a gel. It keeps moisture in your stool and feeds your gut microbiome.
- **Insoluble fiber, the bulker.** It does not dissolve. It bulks up stool and stretches the bowel to trigger movement.
- **Water, the activator.** Water makes fiber work. More fiber without more water can make constipation and bloating worse.

Eat both kinds. Raise your water a little each time you add fiber, and aim for balance: pale yellow urine. And go slow: add only a few grams a week, so your body adjusts and bloating stays low.

**Try it today: the kiwi start**

1. Eat 1 large kiwi. That is 3 grams of fiber, and eating the skin boosts it.
2. Drink 1 extra glass of water with it.
3. Keep it up all week. Next week, add a few more grams.

Kiwi also has actinidin, an enzyme that helps constipation [1].

**A question I hear in clinic**

"Can't I just take a fiber supplement?"

Food first. The best health data is on fiber from food. One serving of psyllium gives 4 to 6 grams, far from 25. Psyllium is the most studied supplement, and it helps in some cases, like constipation. Ask your doctor before you start one, especially with a bowel condition, a wheat allergy or celiac disease.
