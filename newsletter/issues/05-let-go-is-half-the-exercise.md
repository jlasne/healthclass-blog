Hi {{first_name|there}},

Every rep I teach has 2 halves: the lift and the let-go. I time the let-go at twice the lift.

Make a fist and hold it all day. By evening, your grip is weak. Your pelvic floor works the same way.

The International Continence Society lists relaxation as part of normal function, rated full, partial or delayed [1].

Some pelvic problems come from too much tension. A systematic review found signs of raised tone or overactive pelvic floor muscles across several pelvic health conditions [2]. For those women, more squeezing adds to the problem. A better let-go is the fix.

"100 Kegels a day" is a myth. Guidelines set a floor of 8 contractions, 3 times a day [3]. Each one gets a full let-go. 24 good reps beat 100 rushed ones.

**Try it today: the 2-minute let-go drill**

1. Lie on your back with a pillow under your knees.
2. Breathe in through your nose for 4 seconds. Let your belly rise and your pelvic floor soften, like a hammock taking weight.
3. Breathe out for 6 seconds. Do nothing else.
4. Scan from the top: jaw soft, shoulders heavy, belly soft, pelvic floor soft.

Repeat for 8 breaths.

**A question I hear in clinic**

"How long should I rest between Kegels?"

While you learn, let go for twice as long as you lift: a 2-second lift, then a 4-second let-go. As your holds reach 5 to 10 seconds, let go for at least as long as you held. After each set, take 1 extra breath, only for letting go.

**See a doctor or a pelvic floor physical therapist first if you have**

- Pain with sex or with tampons.
- Trouble starting to pee, or a slow stream.
- A bladder that still feels full after you go.
- Constipation, with straining.
- Pelvic, low back or hip pain that gets worse after Kegels.
