Hi {{first_name|there}},

In my first letter, I said your type of leak decides your first step. Here is a quick way to find it.

Leaks come in 2 main patterns:

- **Stress leaks** happen with effort. A cough, a sneeze, a laugh or a heavy bag.
- **Urge leaks** come with a sudden, strong need to go. The leak follows, on the way to the toilet.
- **Mixed leaks** are both.

Among US women who leak, 37.5% have stress leaks only, 22% have urge leaks only and 31.3% have both [1].

Our site now has a free 3-question quiz about when you leak. Your result shows on the page, with no email.

What to do with your result:

- **Stress leaks:** guidelines list pelvic floor training for at least 3 months as the first step [2]. The Knack, a squeeze timed before a sneeze, helps on the spot.
- **Urge leaks:** see your doctor first. Bladder habits help many women: what you drink, when you go, and how you calm an urge [3].
- **Mixed leaks:** start with the one that bothers you most.

**Try it today: 3 days of leak notes**

For 3 days, write down each leak: the time, what you were doing, and whether an urge came first. Bring the notes to your doctor. They help more than any quiz.

**A question I hear in clinic**

"Can a quiz really tell me which leak I have?"

It can point to a pattern. The quiz follows the shape of the 3 Incontinence Questions, from a 2006 study of 301 women. They found 86% of the stress leaks and 75% of the urge leaks that a full doctor's evaluation found [4]. So about 1 answer in 4 to 7 lands in the wrong group. It is not a diagnosis: a doctor confirms.

**See a doctor if** you leak with no effort and no urge, or your bladder never feels empty.
